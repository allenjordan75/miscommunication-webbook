## Chapter Setup

### Packet Fidelity Gate

Complete `workflow/templates/packet-fidelity-map-template.md` before drafting this canvas.

Do not draft from this template until:

- the required chapter movement is identified
- section goals are mapped
- required examples are mapped
- allowed metaphors and phrases are listed
- the key-term ledger is complete
- source/example jobs are mapped
- missing support is flagged

The packet is the content authority. This template organizes packet material; it does not authorize new content.

### What Students Think The Topic Is

What will students likely assume about this topic before the chapter?

### What This Chapter Offers As An Alternative

What does this chapter do to provide an alternative to students' current assumptions, habits, or attitudes about the topic? What new way of noticing, interpreting, or responding does it make available?

### Master Annotated Bibliography

Add the chapter's full source pool here before mapping sources into individual sections. For each source, include citation, 2-4 sentence summary, key concepts/findings, what claim it supports, what it does not support, and which Chapter 2 section(s) it may belong in.

Source sync rule: if a source appears in a section-level source box, quote box, uploaded source document, or source map but is missing here, add it here too. Include where the source appeared, what claim it supports, and what citation details are still missing.

## Before You Read

### Opening Paragraph

Introduce the topic through the common-sense approach.

Prompt:

> A common-sense approach often treats [topic] as [familiar assumption]. If [simple condition], then [common-sense conclusion].

### Reframing Paragraph

Name the (un)commonsense approach.

Prompt:

> The (un)commonsense approach asks a different question: [better question about meaning, context, relationship, history, body, culture, power, technology, or situation].

### Big Idea

Write one student-facing claim that carries the chapter.

Big Idea:

> [Topic] is not simply [common-sense version]. It takes shape through [specific processes]. [Common-sense value] still matters, but it cannot carry the whole burden of understanding.

## After You Read

### Reflection Prompt

Ask students to identify one concrete moment where the chapter topic felt obvious but was shaped by position, expectation, relationship, history, language, technology, culture, power, or situation.

Prompt:

> Before moving to the quiz, pause and identify one moment from the chapter where [topic] felt obvious to someone but was still shaped by [chapter-specific conditions].

### Quick Check

Ask students to explain the chapter's central contrast.

Prompt:

> Be ready to explain the difference between asking, "[common-sense question]?" and asking, "[uncommonsense question]?"
