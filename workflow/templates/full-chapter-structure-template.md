# Full Chapter Structure Template

Use this structure for Chapter 3 and future full chapters unless Jordan gives a chapter-specific structure. This template preserves the Chapter 2 architecture while giving future chapters enough room for foundations, types/processes, approaches, leaps, practice, and back matter.

The packet is still the content authority. This template organizes packet material; it does not authorize new concepts, vocabulary, metaphors, examples, claims, or sources.

## Review Before You Begin

**Purpose:** Reconnect students to the relevant earlier framework without over-explaining it.

Use this section to:

- remind students of the prior framework needed for this chapter
- clarify that the chapter is not rejecting the older value, concept, or practice
- prepare students for the chapter's central movement

For many chapters, this will briefly reconnect to knowing vs. understanding.

## Chapter Overview

**Purpose:** Give students the map before the opening story.

Preview:

- the chapter topic
- the common-sense assumption
- the (un)commonsense reframing
- the major concepts/processes students will learn
- the practical stakes for interpersonal communicating

## Learning Objectives

Use student-facing objectives. Objectives should define, compare, explain, and practice the chapter's core moves.

## 1. Opening Story / Opening Case

**Purpose:** Make the chapter problem felt before defining it.

Use an author-provided story, case, example, or packet anchor when available. Do not invent a new opening case if the packet already provides one.

This section should:

- make the topic emotionally or practically recognizable
- introduce the common-sense assumption before naming it
- end by pointing to the larger chapter problem

## 1.1 Preview: The Bigger Question

**Purpose:** Turn the story into the chapter problem.

This section should work like Chapter 2's preview after The Dress: do not stay at the level of the story. Ask what the story reveals about the larger topic, certainty, common sense, understanding, or interpersonal communicating.

## 2. Why This Topic Belongs in Interpersonal Communicating

**Purpose:** Show students why the chapter belongs in an interpersonal communication textbook.

Use this section to move the topic out of abstraction and into everyday life. Include packet-supported examples showing how the chapter topic affects relationships, bodies, behavior, credibility, identity, conflict, workplaces, technology, families, friendships, or ordinary judgment.

## 3. Foundations: What The Core Concept Is And How It Works

**Purpose:** Give students the basic vocabulary, process, and conceptual foundation.

This section should define the chapter's core object and explain enough process for students to understand what they are looking at. It should not become a technical detour.

Possible subsection pattern:

- 3.1 What [Core Concept] Is
- 3.2 Anatomy / Parts / Process / Basic Structure
- 3.3 How The Process Works
- 3.4 What The Process Does And Does Not Guarantee

Use packet-based definitions. Do not use dictionary definitions unless the packet asks for them.

## 4. Types / Forms / Major Variations

**Purpose:** Help students understand that the chapter topic is not one thing.

Use this section when the packet distinguishes types, forms, approaches, genres, modes, or variations. Do not rank types from best to worst unless the packet explicitly asks for that.

Each type should include:

- What it is
- What it accomplishes
- A packet-supported interpersonal example
- A student-facing hook or takeaway

## 5. How The Topic Moves Into Everyday Life

**Purpose:** Bridge foundations to the approach framework.

Use this section to show how the chapter topic leaves the classroom, article, concept, or formal setting and becomes part of everyday interpretation, advice, habits, relationships, workplace expectations, media scripts, AI answers, or interpersonal judgment.

This section should be example-rich and packet-grounded.

## 6. Chapter Movement Header

Use the chapter's exact movement as the header when possible.

Example pattern:

- From Certainty to Curiosity
- From Accuracy to Sense-Making
- From [common-sense framing] to [uncommonsense reframing]

Begin with a transition paragraph that asks what students expect the chapter topic to do and prepares the approach contrast.

## 6.1 The Common-Sense Approach To [Topic]

**Purpose:** Define the common-sense approach before the leaps.

This section should:

- define how the common-sense approach treats the topic
- explain why that approach feels useful
- identify what the approach helps students do
- preview the common-sense assumptions the leaps will complicate
- show where the approach becomes too small

Do not make the common-sense approach sound stupid. The problem is usually not that common sense is useless. The problem is that common sense becomes too small when it is the only framework students have.

## 6.2 The (Un)Commonsense Approach To [Topic]

**Purpose:** Define the alternative before the leaps.

This section should:

- define how the (un)commonsense approach treats the topic
- show what questions become available
- preview the (un)commonsense assumptions the leaps will develop
- connect the approach to responsibility, context, relationship, uncertainty, and better judgment

The (un)commonsense approach should be a practice, not just a nicer label.

## 7. Discontinuous Leaps

**Purpose:** Introduce the leap structure before the three leap sections.

Briefly remind students that a discontinuous leap is a shift in the assumption underneath a habit, not a tiny upgrade to the same habit.

Use exactly three named leaps unless Jordan approves a different number.

## 8. Practice Framework / Application Moves

**Purpose:** Give students something they can actually do.

Use the packet's named practice framework when one exists. If the packet does not provide a named practice framework, use packet-supported application moves without inventing a new framework.

Each practice move should include:

- what the move means
- why it matters
- a practical question
- a packet-supported example or application

## 9. Chapter Recap

Use the Chapter 0/Chapter 2 recap style.

Recommended recap pattern:

- We started with:
- We complicated:
- Concept to remember:
- Everyday application:

## 10. Key Terms At A Glance

Include required terms from the packet. Define them in first-year language using packet-based definitions.

Each definition should explain why the term matters for this chapter.

Publication standard: key terms introduced in the chapter body should function as clickable definition buttons, following the Chapter 2 pattern. Use the Key Terms At A Glance section as the single source for definitions so students can click terms in context without duplicating or drifting definitions.

## 11. Discussion Questions

Keep discussion questions practical and reflective. Ask students to apply the chapter to everyday interactions, advice, social media claims, classroom/workplace situations, relationships, or chapter-specific examples.

## 12. Endnotes / References

Use endnotes rather than APA in-text citations unless Jordan asks otherwise.

Rules:

- research claims need endnotes
- every endnote must be used
- do not invent sources
- do not stretch sources beyond what the packet says they support

## Clean Numbered Outline

When drafting a new chapter, end the planning stage with a simple numbered outline matching the final chapter structure.
