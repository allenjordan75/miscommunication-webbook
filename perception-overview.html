Perception

How we notice, interpret, and respond to interpersonal life

Overview

In this module, we will explore how perception shapes interpersonal communicating. Perception is not just a private mental process that happens before communication begins. It is part of how we participate in relationships, interpret other people, make judgments, respond to conflict, and decide what a situation means.

The module moves from discussion, to reading, to quiz, to application. Along the way, you will keep returning to a central question: what happens when people are inside the same interaction but are not perceiving the same thing?

What You Will Do

1

Discussion: Perception and Interpersonal Communicating

First, you will explore how perception shapes interpersonal communicating in a discussion. The goal is to start with ordinary experience: moments when people saw the same situation differently, made different assumptions, or walked away with different meanings.

2

Reading: The Perception Chapter

Next, you will read the perception chapter. The chapter contrasts the common-sense and (un)commonsense models of communicating. Pay attention to how each approach explains perception, misunderstanding, difference, interpretation, and responsibility.

3

Quiz: Perception

After the reading, you will complete a quiz on the perception chapter. The quiz will check your understanding of key terms, major contrasts between the two approaches, and the practical implications of thinking about perception in an (un)commonsense way.

4

Application Assignment: Differences in Perception

Finally, you will complete an application assignment related to perception and how people handle differences in perception within relationships. You will likely be asked to connect chapter ideas to a real or realistic interpersonal situation where people understand the same moment differently.

Keep In Mind

This module is not asking you to decide who perceived a situation correctly and who perceived it incorrectly. Sometimes that question matters. But interpersonal communicating often requires a more careful question: how are different perceptions shaping what people can notice, say, hear, repair, or refuse?

Module Focus

Perception is not just what happens inside one person. It is part of how people make meaning together, especially when relationships ask them to handle difference without pretending everyone sees the same world.
