Core shift

Keep the chapter's job visible

Every major section should answer one question more strongly than the others. When one section tries to answer everything, redundancy appears.

Common sense as knowing

Communication is treated as something solved by locating the right meaning, fact, rule, behavior, or interpretation. Useful, but incomplete.

(Un)common sense as understanding

Communication is treated as situated, relational, contextual, dialectical, and adaptive. Students practice noticing how meanings are made and changed.

Rules to heuristics

The practice move: shift from rigid communication rules toward flexible guides that help students notice, interpret, and respond across contexts.

Part 2

Chapter Architecture

Use this as the default chapter order: Opening Story -> Overview -> Approaching -> Discontinuous Leaps -> Practice -> Closing.

1

Opening Story: The Myth in Motion

Pull students into a vivid scene where the commonsense myth feels obvious before it becomes problematic. Make the myth felt before theory appears.

Target: 650-900 words. Avoid starting with definitions or research summaries.

2

H1: Overview of [Topic]

Define the topic, explain why it matters, and give students the foundations needed before comparing approaches.

Target: 1,200-1,800 words, variable. Avoid fully explaining the approaches or leaps.

3

H1: Approaching [Topic]

Compare common sense as knowing and (un)common sense as understanding. Name origins, appeal, limits, behaviors, and previewed assumptions.

Target: 1,000-1,500 words. Avoid doing the leap examples too early.

4

H1: Discontinuous Leaps

Develop one big shift through three specific assumption changes. Each leap changes what students notice and what they try.

Target: 3,200-4,800 words. Avoid three unrelated mini-chapters.

5

H1: Practice: Rules to Heuristics

Translate understanding into flexible practices students can try across contexts. This is practice, not more content.

Target: 1,100-1,700 words. Avoid scripts, rigid advice, or a new theory section.

6

Closing: What to Carry Forward

Return to the story, consolidate the big shift, name 4-6 key terms, ask reflection questions, and bridge to the next chapter.

Target: 300-500 words. Avoid a heading-by-heading summary.

Part 3

Anti-Redundancy Locks

Use this map when a section starts doing another section's work.

Section Primary job Fix
Opening StoryMake students feel the commonsense myth.Move definitions and research to Overview or Leaps.
OverviewTeach foundations.Use only a light setup; save comparison for Approaching.
ApproachingCompare common sense and (un)common sense.Preview assumptions only; do not develop them.
Discontinuous LeapsTransform assumptions and behaviors.Keep the three myth expressions distinct; do not repeat the same claim in each leap.
PracticeTranslate understanding into flexible practices.Use leaps as source material for heuristics, not recap headings.
ClosingConsolidate and transfer.Return to the anchor story and ask what now looks different.

Paragraph-level check

  • Cut if the paragraph only announces that something matters without showing an example, consequence, or question.
  • Move if the paragraph teaches a concept in Practice that belongs in Overview.
  • Merge if two paragraphs explain the same myth with different wording but no new consequence.
  • Split if one paragraph tries to define, compare, give research, and offer practice all at once.
  • Replace if the paragraph says common sense is wrong without preserving its partial truth.
  • Add if the section has three abstract paragraphs in a row. Add example -> consequence -> question.

Final chapter checklist

Before calling the chapter drafted

  • The title names both the topic and the movement from common sense to (un)common sense.
  • The opening story makes the commonsense myth felt before theory appears.
  • The Overview gives foundations but does not fully compare approaches.
  • The Approaching section answers the required questions for each approach.
  • The chapter has one big shift developed through three discontinuous leaps.
  • The chapter uses three distinct myth expressions, one for each leap.
  • Each leap includes common sense assumption -> myth -> behavior and (un)common sense assumption -> belief/heuristic -> different behavior.
  • Each leap uses a distinct research-based or everyday example.
  • The chapter prioritizes lived experience while using research to legitimize the (un)common sense approach.
  • The Practice section is titled "Practice: Rules to Heuristics."
  • Practice strategies are flexible heuristics, not scripts or universal rules.
  • The closing returns to the opening story and asks what students now notice, understand, and might try differently.
