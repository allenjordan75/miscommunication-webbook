IPC 2110 Personal Chapter Drafting Template

Use this template to plan a full textbook chapter that begins with an opening story, moves from the common-sense approach toward the (un)common-sense approach, and develops three discontinuous leaps supported by sources, quotes, callouts, illustrations, and endnote-ready citations.

How to use this template

  1. Fill the chapter-level setup first. Setup means the topic, the chapter's main movement, what students probably assume now, what the chapter will help them understand differently, and what they should be able to try by the end.
  2. For each chapter section, complete the relevant labeled boxes. Progress means the section has enough goals, claims, examples, sources, callout ideas, and visual notes for AI to draft without guessing.
  3. Use messy-thought boxes for raw voice notes, fragments, partial explanations, and half-formed examples. Use refined-language boxes for sentences or paragraphs you may want preserved closely.
  4. Use annotated bibliography boxes only for sources: citation, summary, relevant concept, supported claim, limits, and citation/endnote notes.
  5. Before building a drafting packet, run the clarifying-questions step so AI reviews the whole workflow and asks only necessary questions.
  6. Use the master AI prompt when you want ChatGPT to draft the full chapter at once, or use the section prompts when you want to work one section at a time.
  7. Use the export buttons to create a Word, HTML, text, or Markdown planning file.

Rules for source materials

  1. Messy notes and voice notes are treated as author intent, possible examples, and conceptual raw material. AI should organize them, not quote them as polished prose unless you mark them as refined language.
  2. Refined ideas and language are treated as preferred wording. AI should preserve phrasing where possible and ask before substantially changing key terms or signature sentences.
  3. Annotated bibliographies are treated as evidence. AI should map each source to a claim, identify citation needs, avoid overstating findings, and flag unsupported claims.
  4. Images and illustration ideas are treated as visual prompts and placement notes. AI should use them to propose figures, captions, alt text, and accessibility guidance.
  5. Callout ideas are treated as optional teaching supports. AI should place them only where they clarify, complicate, define, or help students pause and notice.

Chapter flow

Chapter Architecture

Use this as the default chapter order: Opening Story -> Overview -> Approaches -> Discontinuous Leaps -> Practice -> Closing.

1

Opening Story: The Myth in Motion

Achieve curiosity, relevance, emotional stakes, and a concrete problem that students can recognize before concepts appear.

Section goal: make the common-sense myth felt without resolving it.

2

H1: Overview of [Topic]

Build the shared foundation students need before comparing the two approaches.

Section goal: define, ground, and prepare.

3

H1: Approaching [Topic]

Contrast the common-sense approach as knowing with the (un)common-sense approach as understanding through definitions and operating narratives.

Section goal: compare approaches without doing the leaps yet.

4

H1: Discontinuous Leaps

Develop three assumption shifts that change what students notice, believe, and do.

Section goal: transform assumptions into behavioral consequences.

5

H1: Practice Bridge

Bridge a common-sense reaction into an (un)common-sense response.

Section goal: make the conceptual shift practicable.

6

Closing: What to Carry Forward

Return to the opening story and consolidate what students can now notice, understand, and try.

Section goal: synthesize, transfer, and motivate continued practice.

Quality control

Final Chapter Checks

Use this list before drafting, revising, or asking ChatGPT to review the chapter.

  • The chapter begins with the opening story, not a front-matter overview.
  • Every section has a fixed section goal that stays consistent across chapters.
  • Overview and Practice use a section claim plus expandable main idea boxes with evidence/development and callout ideas.
  • Every section keeps direct quotes and a specific help request box.
  • Every AI prompt focuses on goals, outcomes, rhetorical moves, transitions, repetition checks, endnotes, voice, callouts, illustrations, images, captions, videos, and embeds.
  • The two approach overview boxes define and narrate the common-sense approach and the (un)common-sense approach before the leaps begin.
  • There are exactly three discontinuous leaps.
  • Each leap includes common-sense assumption, myth, communication impact, and potential examples.
  • Each leap includes (un)common-sense assumption, belief, behavior change, and potential examples.
  • The Practice section bridges one common-sense practice to one (un)common-sense practice through a small example narrative.
  • The comparative illustration appears near the end and draws from the leaps with accessibility guidance for screen readers and text tools.
  • Videos and embeds include URLs or embed codes, exact placement notes, instructor notes, and accessibility/status notes.
  • Images include file paths or URLs, captions, exact placement notes, instructor notes, and accessibility/status notes.
  • The prompt pack at the bottom lets selected prompts run together and includes the Chapter 0 voice anchor as a secondary style guide.
  • The master draft prompt asks for Word-ready output with editable titles, headers, endnotes, callout recommendations, and key-term callout boxes.
  • Drafted citations appear as endnotes.
