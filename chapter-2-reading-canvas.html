Chapter 2 Reading

Perception (Mis)Perception

From accuracy to sense-making

Before You Read

This chapter asks you to rethink perception. A common-sense approach often treats perception as accurate knowing: if I saw it, heard it, or felt it clearly, then I know what happened. The (un)commonsense approach asks a different question: how did this perception become meaningful from where someone stands?

Big Idea

Perception rarely arrives as raw reality. It takes shape through noticing, grouping, meaning-making, and story-building. Accuracy still matters, but it cannot carry the whole burden of understanding.

Read For These Moves

  • The DressNotice how a disagreement about color becomes a larger problem about certainty, reality, and how quickly perception can become proof.
  • The perceptual loopTrack how selection, organization, interpretation, and storying work together. The chapter treats perception as a living loop, not a clean pipeline.
  • Common sense vs. (un)commonsenseWatch how the common-sense model asks whether perception accurately represents reality, while the (un)commonsense model asks how perception becomes meaningful from a body, history, relationship, culture, language, technology, and situation.
  • Relational responsibilityPay attention to what changes when perceptual difference is not automatically treated as error, irrationality, or bad faith.

The Three Named Leaps

  • Leap 1: From Linear Processing to Recursive Sense-MakingPerception is not a simple input-processing-output machine. It is a loop where prior meanings shape what people notice next, while new evidence, interaction, and perspective can revise those meanings.
  • Leap 2: From Private Mental Process to Collaborative Sense-MakingPerception does not happen only inside isolated minds. People make sense through bodies, relationships, tools, shared attention, histories, and environments.
  • Leap 3: From Bias as Error to Bias as Monitored Blind SpotBias is not something only other people have. Every perceptual position has boundaries, so responsible communicating requires checking what our position makes easy and hard to see.

Key Terms To Track

  • PerceptionThe ongoing process of noticing, grouping, meaning-making, and storying experience from a particular position so people can act, communicate, and understand.
  • SelectionThe filtering process that makes some cues available for attention while other cues stay in the background.
  • OrganizationThe patterning process that links selected cues into figures, wholes, categories, sequences, or relationships.
  • InterpretationThe meaning-making process that explains what selected and organized cues suggest within a situation.
  • StoryingThe narrative process that turns interpreted moments into larger accounts of what happened, who people are, and what the event says about a relationship or world.
  • Naive realismThe assumption that we perceive the world more or less as it really is and that reasonable people should see the same reality.

After You Read

Before moving to the quiz, pause and identify one moment from the chapter where perception felt obvious to someone but was still shaped by position, history, expectation, or relationship.

Quick Check

Be ready to explain the difference between asking, "Whose perception is accurate?" and asking, "How did each perception become meaningful from where each person stands?"
